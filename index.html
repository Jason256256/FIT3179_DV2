Section 1: Enrolment Rates in Australia

This section explores early childhood education uptake across various community remoteness levels, gender divergence via difference mapping, and state-level geographic disparities.

Key Insights
  • Remoteness Gap: Participation drops sharply from ~90-95% in Major Cities down to ~58-68% in Very Remote regions.
  • Gender Trend: Female enrolment consistently leads male participation by ~1.3% nationwide across 2016-2026.
  • State Disparity: The ACT and southeastern states lead national rates (>90%), while the Northern Territory lags at ~80%.

Section 2: Attendance Hours in Australia

This section examines average weekly hours spent in early childhood education across community remoteness levels, genders, and regional jurisdictions.

Key Insights
  • Remoteness Gap: Children in Major Cities attend ~6.4 hours/weekday compared to ~4.1 hours in Very Remote areas.
  • Gender Trend: Females consistently have longer attendence times, albeit slightly than males and higher remoteness also correlates to shorter attendance time
  • Regional Leaders: ACT consistently records the highest average weekday participation durations nationwide, followed by Victoria.

Section 3: Enrolment vs Attendance in Australia

This section investigates attendance-to-enrolment ratios across regional communities and shifts in family child-care preferences across facility types.

7. Attendance-to-Enrolment Ratio by Community

8. Share of Children by Facility Type (2016 vs 2026)
Key Insights
  • Efficiency Gradient: Cities peak at 96% efficiency, dropping to a 68% peak in Very Remote areas.
  • Facility Dominance: Long Day Care (LDC) leads, growing from 52% to 58.5%.
  • Preschool Trend: Standalone Kindergartens/Preschools show a moderate relative share decline.
  • Lowest Shares: In-Home Care remains lowest at 3%, followed by Family Day Care at ~8%.

Section 4: Australia vs Malaysia Comparison

This section compares trends in enrolment rates between Australia and Malaysia, by community type (urban or rural), gender (male or female) and states.

Key Insights
  • Urban Advantage: Both Australia and Malaysia maintain higher enrolment rates in urban centers compared to rural communities.
  • Gender Balance: Enrolment proportions across genders remain relatively balanced near 50% for both nations.
  • Regional Variation: Capital territories and metropolitan regions (such as Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya) consistently achieve the highest participation rates.
